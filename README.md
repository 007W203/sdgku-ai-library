# SDGKU AI Research Reference Library

## Purpose

This folder is the master research-reference library for SDGKU coursework.

An AI assisting with SDGKU coursework should read this file first.

The files in `Sources` describe research databases, scholarly resources,
official organizations, writing resources, and citation tools available
to the student.

This library tells an AI:

- where useful research may be found
- which sources are appropriate for different subjects
- which sources should generally receive priority
- how to search effectively
- how to handle citations
- how to distinguish retrieved evidence from analysis

---

# Core Research Rules

When assisting with an SDGKU assignment:

1. Read this README first.

2. Identify the subject and research requirements of the assignment.

3. Review the relevant source files in the `Sources` directory.

4. Prefer authoritative and academically appropriate material.

5. Prefer peer-reviewed sources when an assignment requires scholarly research.

6. Prefer primary or official sources when researching:
   - government
   - international organizations
   - statistics
   - policy
   - trade
   - institutional information

7. Never invent:
   - authors
   - article titles
   - quotations
   - journal names
   - publication dates
   - page numbers
   - DOIs
   - URLs
   - statistics
   - citation information

8. Never claim to have accessed a database, article, book, video,
   or document unless its content was actually retrieved.

9. If a source requires SDGKU authentication and access is unavailable,
   state that authentication is required.

10. Clearly distinguish:
    - information directly supported by a source
    - interpretation or analysis
    - inference
    - general background knowledge

11. Preserve complete citation information whenever available.

12. Prefer stable URLs or DOIs over temporary session URLs.

13. Save useful retrieved materials and research notes in the appropriate
    Research folders.

# Preferred Research Order

The exact source depends on the assignment, but use this general priority:

1. SDGKU institutional academic databases
2. Peer-reviewed scholarly databases
3. Official international or government sources
4. Primary sources
5. Academic books and journals
6. Google Scholar or other scholarly discovery tools when appropriate
7. Reputable professional and industry sources
8. General web sources when academically appropriate

# Source Selection Guide

## Business, Management, Finance, Marketing, Economics

Start with:

- EBSCO Business Source Elite
- EBSCO Regional Business News
- JSTOR

Also consider:

- UNCTAD for international trade and development
- official company or government sources when primary information is needed

## Global Business and International Trade

Start with:

- UNCTAD
- EBSCO
- JSTOR

Use UNCTAD especially for:

- international trade
- development
- investment
- globalization
- international economic policy

## History, Humanities and Social Sciences

Start with:

- JSTOR
- EBSCO

## Latin America or Spanish-Language Scholarly Research

Start with:

- REDALYC

REDALYC is especially valuable for peer-reviewed research from
Latin America, Spain and Portugal.

## Education, Culture, Science and International Programs

Start with:

- UNESCO

## Global Systems and Global Dynamics Video Research

Use:

- International Training Center

## APA Style, Writing and Citation Assistance

Use:

- Purdue OWL
- Zotero
- Citation Machine

Purdue OWL should generally be preferred for APA formatting guidance.

Zotero is useful for managing research sources and bibliographic metadata.

Citation generators should be checked for accuracy before citations
are submitted in academic work.

# Search Strategy

Use focused academic search terms instead of full natural-language questions
when searching research databases.

## Boolean Operators

### AND

Narrows results by requiring both concepts.

Example:

    climate change AND Alaska

### OR

Broadens a search by including alternative terms.

Example:

    entrepreneur OR entrepreneurship

### NOT

Excludes an unwanted concept.

Example:

    artificial intelligence NOT robotics

## Useful Search Techniques

Use quotation marks for exact phrases:

    "global supply chain"

Combine concepts:

    "global supply chain" AND resilience

Use alternatives:

    entrepreneurship AND (Mexico OR "Latin America")

Use exclusions carefully:

    tourism AND sustainability NOT cruise

When available, use database filters for:

- Peer Reviewed
- Full Text
- Publication Date
- Subject
- Academic Journals
- Language

---

# Citation Capture Rule

When a useful source is found, preserve as much of the following as available:

- Author or organization
- Article or document title
- Journal or publication
- Publication date
- Volume
- Issue
- Page range
- Publisher
- DOI
- Stable URL
- Database name
- Date accessed when required

Do this while researching rather than trying to reconstruct citations later.

# Research Storage

## Articles

`Research\Articles`

Store downloaded:

- PDFs
- journal articles
- reports
- books or book chapters when permitted
- primary-source documents

## Notes

`Research\Notes`

Store:

- research summaries
- source notes
- quotation notes
- evidence tables
- topic notes

Notes should identify the source they came from.

## Citations

`Research\Citations`

Store:

- reference lists
- bibliographies
- citation exports
- Zotero exports
- APA reference drafts

## Source Exports

`Research\Source-Exports`

Store exports obtained directly from research databases, such as:

- RIS
- BibTeX
- citation downloads
- database result exports
- metadata files

# Important AI Instruction

This reference library is a directory and research guide.

A link appearing in this library does NOT mean the linked content has
automatically been retrieved.

The AI must distinguish between:

1. knowing that a source exists
2. accessing the source
3. reading the source
4. using evidence from the source

Only information actually retrieved from a source should be attributed
to that source.
